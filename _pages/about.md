Welcome! I am currently a PhD candidate in the Department of Sociology at NYU. I am a quantitative sociologist studying the institutions, markets, and policies that generate and sustain racial and spatial inequality. I pursue this agenda by studying key eras of transformation in American housing. My current projects focus on (1) the New Deal-era construction of the American homeownership society and (2) the emergence of climate risk as a force reshaping residential mobility. 

My dissertation examines the role of climate risk in residential mobility. I show how racialized and classed patterns of residential mobility into and out of flood-prone neighborhoods are reshaping the geography of environmental inequality and segregation. One paper from this project is [published in *Demography*](https://read.dukeupress.edu/demography/article/doi/10.1215/00703370-12788356/412382/Countering-the-Current-Flood-Risk-and-Uneven) and received an honorable mention from the ASA Race, Gender, and Class Section for best graduate student paper.

In a second collaborative project, I study how federal mortgage programs shaped racial inequality in homeownership and neighborhood outcomes during the mid-twentieth century. I co-founded the Redlining Lab, a research group supported by the National Science Foundation, Russell Sage Foundation, Washington Center for Equitable Growth, Social Science Research Council, and other funders. We are building new archival datasets to document the lending patterns and long-term consequences of Federal Housing Administration (FHA) and Veterans Administration (VA) lending. Our first paper from this project is [published in *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10850-8). 

Before NYU, I worked at the Urban Institute as a Research Analyst. I received my B.A. from Rice University in Statistics and Policy Studies. 

My CV is available [here](https://katherineathomas.github.io/files/Katherine_Thomas_CV.pdf).
